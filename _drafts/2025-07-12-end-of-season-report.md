- Initially started with no enrichments; only cleared some grass around the vines. Experiment to see how they would naturally fare here.
- Wanted to see how they would naturally grow
- dug at 30cm with earth auger according to instruction

- Encouragingly, no apparent mildew, rot, aphids, or pests.
- Discovered that we have another random 20 year old white variety in the garden. Looks very healthy, but no clue if it is suitable for wine. Will cut back almost everything in the winter and try to properly train it. Doesn't seem like it was grafted. Seeing it being healthy with zero special care is encouraging.
- Velcro tied against bamboo stakes in late june
- In late june and july, there was some browning on the leaves. Possibly due to drought or a lack of magnesium.
- Tested the soil in mid July. As suspected, the soil is nutritionally poor and acid (~4.5 pH). This was my first time using a soil testing kit though so not sure how accurate it is.
- Initial growth was very promising, but stagnation around July. Seeing them so small made me sad, so decided to go for the terraforming route anyway.
- Grass probably ate too much water and nitrogen. Could be the reason for the slow growth.

- My initial plan was to sow a lot of white clover (latin name), but in hindsight I feared it may be too risky with these young vines plus weed control. Opted for wood chips as mulch instead.

## Mid summer terraforming

Done in mid July, took the entire damn weekend.

The soil was terraformed with:

- 20kg of lava grit
- 20kg of lava meal
- 3.5kg of bone meal
- 5kg of bentonite
- 40L of bio compost
- 10kg of chalk pellets
- And 420L of bio planting soil to top it all off

Behold this Holy Dry Mix here:

![Holy Dry Mix](/images/holy_dry_mix.webp "Holy Dry Mix")

Mulching was done at 2025-08-04 with 500l. Don't get mulch from the Praxis; it contains some traces plastic and glass.
I scattered white clover before I decided I would just mulch instead. Although I attempted to cover the clover completely, those pesky bastards seem to grow straight through the mulch. Ah well.
